01 / THE ROAD TO WAR

Six years that changed everything

The October War did not begin on the morning of 6 October. Its roots lay in the 1967 war, the loss of Sinai, and years of failed diplomacy and military preparation.

01JUNE 1967

A changed map

In the Six-Day War, Israel captured the Sinai Peninsula from Egypt. The loss reshaped Egyptian politics and left the canal front as a heavily fortified line.

021967–1970

The War of Attrition

Along the Suez Canal, artillery exchanges and raids continued through a grinding conflict. A 1970 ceasefire paused the fighting, but the political dispute remained unresolved.

031971–1973

Preparation and planning

President Anwar Sadat pursued diplomatic initiatives while Egypt rebuilt its armed forces. Planners developed a limited objective: cross the canal, break the Bar Lev Line, and establish positions in Sinai.

The plan relied on surprise, coordinated arms, and breaching the sand embankment with high-pressure water pumps.

02 / THE CAMPAIGN

Twenty days that altered the map

A concise guide to the opening, the fighting, and the ceasefire. The front shifted as both sides adapted.

06 OCT

The crossing begins

At about 2 p.m., Egyptian forces launched Operation Badr across the Suez Canal as Syrian forces attacked on the Golan Heights. The coordinated assault caught Israel by surprise.

06–08 OCT

Bridges and bridgeheads

Assault troops crossed in boats, opened breaches in the Bar Lev Line, and engineers assembled pontoon bridges. Egyptian forces established positions on the eastern bank.

14 OCT

A major armored battle

Egyptian forces advanced eastward in a large armored offensive. The attack met strong Israeli resistance and heavy losses, and the front settled into a new phase.

15–22 OCT

The front shifts

Israeli forces crossed to the west bank of the canal and encircled Egypt's Third Army. International diplomacy intensified as the military situation became increasingly precarious.

22–25 OCT

Ceasefire and aftermath

UN Security Council resolutions called for a ceasefire. Fighting continued in places before a more durable ceasefire took hold. Disengagement agreements followed in 1974 and 1975.

THE CROSSING / 06 OCTOBER

A breach in the sand. A bridge across the water.

Egyptian engineers used powerful water pumps to cut passages through the Bar Lev Line's sand embankment. Assault troops crossed by boat; pontoon bridges then carried armor and supplies into Sinai. The operation demanded timing, teamwork, and immense logistical effort.

03 / PEOPLE AND LEADERSHIP

Decisions made under pressure

The war was shaped by political choices, military planning, and the actions of people across the front. These figures played prominent roles in its course.

01PRESIDENT OF EGYPT

Anwar
Sadat

Egypt's president from 1970, Sadat authorized the October offensive as part of a strategy to break the political deadlock over occupied Sinai and bring the conflict back to international diplomacy.

02COMMANDER-IN-CHIEF

Ahmed Ismail
Ali

As minister of war and commander-in-chief, Field Marshal Ahmed Ismail Ali oversaw Egypt's military preparations and led the armed forces during the October campaign.

03CHIEF OF STAFF

Saad
El Shazly

Chief of Staff of the Egyptian Armed Forces, Lieutenant General Saad El Shazly was closely associated with planning the canal crossing and the initial bridgehead operation.

04SYRIAN PRESIDENT

Hafez
al-Assad

Syria's president led his country's forces on the northern front. The simultaneous Syrian offensive on the Golan Heights formed the second front of the war.

04 / ACTS OF COURAGE

Courage, measured in teamwork

The crossing depended on thousands of people performing difficult tasks under fire. Many acts of courage were collective: a crew holding a bridge, a unit keeping a foothold, a medic reaching the wounded.

01

The engineers

Under fire, engineering teams opened passages through the sand barrier and assembled bridges that allowed successive waves to cross.

02

The bridge crews

Pontoon crews worked in exposed conditions to connect the banks, keeping vital routes open for troops, armor, and supplies.

03

The defenders

Infantry units held exposed positions in Sinai through counterattacks, helping secure the bridgeheads established in the opening days.

Their story belongs to the soldiers and support crews whose names history did not always record.

05 / THE LEGACY

From battlefield
to negotiating table

The October War ended without a simple military resolution. It changed calculations on every side, renewed negotiations, and led to disengagement agreements. In 1978, Egypt and Israel signed the Camp David Accords; the 1979 peace treaty provided for Israel's withdrawal from Sinai, completed in 1982.
